\label{sec_introduction}

\vspace{0.5cm}\\

In this study, we propose a novel \ac{fe} study of the interaction between a \acf{flu} and a \acf{mem}---a deformable fluid layer whose material elements can flow, and whose elastic energy is given by the Helfrich model \cite{helfrichElasticPropertiesLipid1973}. Our approach is based on the \ac{ale} method \cite{malvernIntroductionMechanicsContinuous1969,howellp.AppliedSolidMechanics2008}. Our analysis  includes a complete description of the physical features of both the membrane and the fluid, it may describe the inertial regime of both, and it accounts for a detailed description of membrane elasticity, rheology, and extensibility. Unlike fluidic approaches where the membrane dynamics is determined by the the fluid, here both the flows and shape of the membrane are determined by intrinsic physical evolution equations. In addition, our study can account for both closed and open surfaces, by leveraging the flexibility of its \ac{fe} formulation \cite{loggAutomatedSolutionDifferential2012}.


We solve for the system rheology by  implementing a splitting scheme \cite{chorinNumericalSolutionNavierStokes1968,loggAutomatedSolutionDifferential2012} for the \ac{ns} equations, applied  simultaneously to the membrane and the fluid flow dynamics, and show  that it is numerically stable under given conditions. As for the membrane shape, we generalize the \ac{al} gauge---previously proposed to describe the steady state of membrane tubes \cite{derenyiFormationInteractionMembrane2002}---to the system dynamics, by means of a time-dependent gauge fixing. Overall, this implementation allows for handling strongly turbulent membrane and fluid flows, as well as large membrane deformations.\vspace{0.5cm}\\

The paper is structured as follows. In \cref{sec_results} we discuss the \ac{ale} method, and present two illustrative examples: The interaction between a fluid and a \ac{bod} in \cref{sec_fl_rigid}, and that between  the fluid and \ac{ela} in \cref{sec_fl_elastic}. In \cref{sec_fl_mem} we present our approach for the interaction between a fluid and a Helfrich membrane. In particular, in \cref{sec_fl_mem_mem,sec_fl_mem_dom,sec_fl_mem_flu}, we discuss the splitting scheme and gauge-fixing procedure for the membrane, \ac{dom} and the fluid, respectively. Finally, \cref{sec_discussion} is devoted to the discussion of the results, and future directions. All acronyms used are listed in \cref{sec_acronyms}.
