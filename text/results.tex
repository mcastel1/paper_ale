\section{Results}\label{sec_results}



In all  systems that we will consider, we will introduce the \ac{ref} and \ac{cur} configuration \cite{kamenskyLectureNotesMAE,landauTheoryElasticity1986,slaughterLinearizedTheoryElasticity2002}, see \cref{fig_ref_curr_rigid}. All quantities relative to the \ac{ref} and \ac{cur} configuration will be denoted by the superscript \ac{ref} and \ac{cur}, respectively.
In the \ac{ref} configuration, the region occupied by \ac{bflu} is described by Cartesian coordinates $\bxr$.
In the \ac{cur} configuration, the region occupied by \ac{bflu} is described by Cartesian coordinates $\bxc$.

The \ac{ref} configuration is related to the \ac{cur} one as follows \cite{kamenskyLectureNotesMAE}, see \cref{fig_ref_curr_rigid}. At  time $t$, the point $\bxc$  corresponds to a point $\bxr$ in the \ac{ref} configuration through the deformation field \cite{landauFluidMechanics1987}
\be\label{eq_def_u}
\budom^t(\bxr) \equiv \bxc - \bxr.
\ee
and the deformation-gradient tensor
\be
\label{eq_def_F}
F_{\alpha \beta}(\bu) \equiv \delta_{\alpha \beta} + \pderr{u^ \alpha}{\beta}.
\ee
In what follows, greek indices $\alpha, \beta, \cdots$ will be used to denote vectors and tensors in  two-dimensional Euclidean space---their position as upper or lower indices is thus immaterial \cite{marchiafavaAppuntiDiGeometria2005,landauTheoryElasticity1986}.

We will characterize  the mapping between $\bxc$ and $\bxr$ configurations in \cref{eq_def_u} with the fields
\begin{align}\label{eq_def_phi}
    \bxc & = \bm{\varphi}^t(\bxr), \\
    \label{eq_def_psi}
    \bxr & = \bm{\psi}^t(\bxc).
\end{align}

We denote the \ac{bflu} velocity and surface tension by $\vfc(\bxc)$ and $\sigmafc(\bxc)$, respectively, where the superscript \ac{cur} specifies that these fields depend on the coordinate $\bxc$ in the \ac{cur} configuration.

For each field $\bvfc({\bxc}), \sigmaf(\bxc)$ which depends on the coordinate $\bxc$ in the \ac{cur} configuration, we introduce its counterpart in the \ac{ref} configuration:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq_vf_ref}
    \bvfr(\bxr, t)    & \equiv {\bm \vfc}( \bm{\varphi}^t(\bxr), t), \\
    \label{eq_sigmaf_ref}
    \sigmafr(\bxr, t) & \equiv  \sigmafc( \bm{\varphi}^t(\bxr), t).
\end{align}

The solution of the interaction dynamics between \ac{bflu} and \ac{rig}, \ac{ela} or \ac{mem} is  involved because of the presence of a moving boundary, cf. \cref{fig_ref_curr_rigid,fig_ref_curr_ela,fig_ref_cur_mem}, respectively. Following the \ac{ale} procedure, we will  bridge between the Lagrangian   description used to describe \ac{rig}, \ac{ela} or \ac{mem}, and the Eulerian description of \ac{bflu}  \cite{kamenskyLectureNotesMAE}. To achieve this, in what follows we will consider the equations of motion for \ac{rig}, \ac{ela} or \ac{mem} and \ac{bflu}---which are naturally formulated the \ac{cur} configuration---and  rewrite them in the \ac{ref} configuration.
